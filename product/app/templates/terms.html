What the service is

It finds job listings, scores them against the profile you give it, looks for a real email address for a person at that employer, and writes a short letter. Depending on your setting it either hands you the letter to send yourself, or sends it from the mailbox you connected.

It applies for jobs. It does not get you one. Nobody can promise that, and a service that implied otherwise would be lying to you.

What it will never do

These are commitments, not aspirations, and each is enforced in code:

Guess an email address. If no real address can be found, no letter is sent. There is no fallback to firstname.lastname@company.com.

Claim a qualification you do not hold. Letters are written from what your CV and answers say.

Write to the same employer twice. One employer, one letter, ever.

Name your current employer to anybody.

Write to anyone on your block list, including anyone who asks you to stop.

Your side

You need to be 16 or over and have the right to work where you are applying.

What you upload has to be true. The letters are written from your CV and your answers, and they go out with your name on them. If your CV claims a ticket you do not hold, this will repeat that claim to an employer, and that is on you.

Upload only your own CV and your own details.

Read your drafts. Letters are written by a language model. It is good and it is not perfect. On the by-hand setting you see every letter before it goes. If you switch automatic sending on, you are choosing to let them go without reading them first, and there is a holding window before each one leaves precisely so you can change your mind.

Do not use this to send anything abusive, misleading, or to anyone who has asked you to stop.

Automatic sending

Off unless you turn it on. When it is on:

There is a daily ceiling on how many letters go out, and a holding window before each one leaves.

Letters send from your mailbox, as you. Your mail provider's own rules apply to that, and a personal address sending a large volume of cold email can be rate-limited or suspended by them. The recommended daily number is set low for that reason. You can raise it; the warning beside the control says what it risks.

Or from an address we issue you, if you would rather keep your own out of it. Then no password of yours is held, and your own address cannot be damaged by anything this does. Two things to know: replies still reach you, because your real address is on Reply-To; and everyone using an issued address draws on one daily allowance, so on a busy day some of your letters wait until tomorrow.

If your mail provider rejects the password, automatic sending switches off rather than retrying, because repeatedly failing to authenticate is how an account gets locked.

Withdraw it whenever you like, from your account page. Disconnecting the mailbox deletes the stored credential.
