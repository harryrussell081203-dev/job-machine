The finding, in one table

Every email below used the same rules, the same structure and the same sign-off. The only thing that varied was who received it.

Who you reachedSentRepliedRate
A hiring inbox (careers@, hr@)10550%
A named human (j.smith@)341338%
A generic inbox (info@, hello@)42410%
Everything862226%

Reaching a person or a hiring inbox rather than a generic one was worth roughly four times the reply rate, 41% against 10%. That comparison rests on 44 emails to humans against 42 to generic inboxes, which is a gap far too wide to be noise.

The 50% row is ten emails. Read it as "about as good as a named person", not as better than one.

Does it annoy them?

Out of 86, none of the replies were a complaint about being emailed. The ones that said no said no, which is still more than a portal gives you. What does annoy people is volume and repetition, so the rules that go with this are one email per employer ever, and stop everything the moment anyone replies, including an automated reply.

Do both

Apply through the portal as well. It costs ten minutes and it is where the paperwork ends up anyway. The email is not a replacement for the application, it is the part that makes somebody aware the application exists.

When not to

  • The advert says no direct approaches. Some agencies and public sector roles say so outright. Respect it.
  • You cannot find a real address. A guessed one bounces, and a bounce costs you deliverability on every email after it.
  • The advert is old. Anything over a week has a shortlist already. Under 48 hours is where this works.

Timing

Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 11am, replied best. Monday is triage and Friday is gone.
