The advert is the first place to look and the company's own website is the second, and the second is where most real addresses actually come from.

The five pages worth checking

/contact, /careers, /about, /team, /jobs. A firm of thirty people often has the whole team on one page with addresses next to the names. Larger companies hide it better but usually still publish a recruitment inbox.

Then stop, properly

If nothing real turns up, apply through the portal and move on. Do not work out whether the company uses one address pattern or another and apply it to a name you found on LinkedIn. Three reasons:

  • A bounce is not free. Bounce rate is part of how mailbox providers decide whether your mail reaches inboxes at all. Burn it and your later emails land in spam, including your reply to the employer who did want to talk to you.
  • Verifiers are not certain. Catch-all domains accept everything, so a verifier returns "valid" for an address belonging to nobody.
  • A wrong guess is a cold email about a job sent to somebody who cannot act on it and now thinks less of you.
