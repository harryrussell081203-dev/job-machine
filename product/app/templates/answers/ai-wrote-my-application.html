The stock phrases

23 of them, plus three formatting habits, and the list is stable enough to check a draft against mechanically:

I hope this email finds you well · passionate · leverage · delve · seamless · synergy · dynamic · thrilled · excited to apply · perfect fit · hit the ground running · fast-paced environment · proven track record · results-driven · detail-oriented · team player · I am writing to · utilize · spearheaded · esteemed · keen to · furthermore · moreover

Also out: exclamation marks, em dashes, and any markdown formatting. All three are tells in an email that is supposed to have been typed by a person.

Asking a model to avoid them does not work

It complies for two paragraphs and drifts back, because the banned phrases are what enthusiasm looks like in its training data. The rule has to be enforced rather than requested: check the finished draft against the list and rewrite anything that hits. Doing that automatically is most of what the software behind this site is for.

The risk that actually matters

Style is the small problem. Asked to sell you, a model reaches for credentials you never mentioned, because that is what selling looks like in the text it learned from. It will write that you hold a security clearance because you said Royal Navy, or a ticket because you named the industry.

That gets found at vetting, and it is the one mistake here that follows you. The fix is to write down what is not true about you before you draft anything — a ticket that lapsed, a degree you paused, a clearance that expired, a licence you are working towards — and check every draft against that list as well.

What to keep

Plain English you would say out loud. Confident, warm, slightly informal, no grovelling. The test is whether a tradesman would read it aloud without wincing.
