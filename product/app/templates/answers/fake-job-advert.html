Signs to stop and check

  • Any request for money. Training fees, a DBS check you pay the recruiter for, starter kits, uniforms, visa costs. A real employer does not charge you to work for them.
  • A job offer without an interview, or an "interview" that is only a text chat.
  • Pay that is too good for simple work from home, or payment through your own bank account to "process" money for them.
  • Contact only by WhatsApp, Telegram or a free email address (gmail, outlook) for a company that should have its own.
  • Your bank details, passport or National Insurance number asked for before a written offer.
  • Pressure to decide today.

How to check an employer is real

  1. Search the company on Companies House. Look for an active company with a matching name and address.
  2. Find the company's own website yourself, not through a link in the advert, and check the job is on it or that the contact details match.
  3. Contact them through the details on their own site and ask whether the vacancy is real.

Where to report it

  • JobsAware, the UK's free service for job scams and unfair recruitment.
  • Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, report fraud to Police Scotland on 101.
  • The job board it was on, so they can take it down.
