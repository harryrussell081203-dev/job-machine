In order, best odds first

1. The advert itself

Read it to the last line. A lot of adverts, especially from smaller firms and agencies, print a contact address at the bottom under the salary and the reference number. Almost nobody uses it, because almost everybody clicks Apply.

Nine emails is a small sample and 67% will not hold at scale. The reason it is first anyway is that when an employer prints an address, they are telling you how they want to be contacted.

2. The company's own website

/contact, /careers, /about, /team, /jobs. A firm of thirty people often has the whole team on one page with addresses. This is where most real addresses come from.

3. A hiring inbox, if no name is going

careers@, hr@ and recruitment@ are read by somebody whose job is to read them. info@ is read by nobody in particular and replied at 10%.

Do not guess the pattern

Most advice on this question tells you to work out whether the company uses firstname.lastname@ or flastname@, apply it to the hiring manager's name, and run it through a verifier. The companies giving that advice sell the verifier.

Three reasons not to:

  • A bounce is not free. Your bounce rate is part of how mailbox providers decide whether your mail reaches inboxes at all. Burn it and your later emails land in spam, including your reply to the employer who did want to talk to you.
  • Verifiers are not certain. Catch-all domains accept everything, so a verifier returns "valid" for an address that belongs to nobody. A large share of small-company domains are catch-all.
  • A guess that lands on the wrong person is a cold email about a job sent to somebody who cannot act on it and now thinks less of you.

If no real address can be found, send nothing. Out of the listings processed for the numbers on this site, 516 ended that way, and that is a correct outcome rather than a failure.
