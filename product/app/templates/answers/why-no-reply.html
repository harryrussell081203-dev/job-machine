What actually happens to a portal application

It lands in an applicant tracking system as a row. No email is sent to anybody. If the employer is getting 300 of them, somebody opens the list when they have an afternoon free, and most rows are never opened at all. There is no thread, so even a reader who likes your CV has nothing to reply to.

That is not the employer being rude. It is the format.

What happens when the same employer gets an email

An email arrives in an inbox a person is already reading, and replying is one tap. Here is what that was worth across 86 cold emails to UK employers:

Who was reachedSentRepliedRate
Address printed in the advert9667%
A hiring inbox (careers@, hr@)10550%
A named human341338%
A generic info@42410%
Everything862226%

Two honest caveats. The hiring-inbox row is only ten emails, so read 50% as "about as good as a named person", not as better than one. And these are engineering and technician roles in Scotland.

The comparison I cannot give you

There is no clean number here for portal applications, because portals do not tell you. No read receipt, no rejection in most cases, nothing to count. Anybody quoting you a precise reply rate for applying through a portal has made it up. What can be said is that 26% of these emails got an answer, and that cold email normally runs between 1% and 5%.

What to change

  1. Read the advert to the bottom. If there is an address in it, use that. It replied at 67%.
  2. No address in the advert: try the company's own site. Small firms put the whole team on /contact or /about.
  3. No name going: a hiring inbox beats a generic one by four to one.
  4. Found nothing real? Send nothing. A guessed address bounces, and a bounce costs you the sender reputation that delivers the next thirty.
  5. Apply through the portal as well. It costs nothing.
