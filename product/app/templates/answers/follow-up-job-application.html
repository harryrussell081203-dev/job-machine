The three touches

Three messages to one inbox capture around 93% of the replies a sequence will ever earn. Past that the curve is flat and the cost is not.

  • Day 4 — a short nudge on the same thread. No attachment, two or three lines, no new argument.
  • Day 9 — one last nudge, same thread.
  • Day 16 — only if you know a second real address at that company. A first email to a new person is a new conversation, which is the one good reason to go past three.

Any reply stops everything

Including an automated one. An autoresponder means the address works and a human will see the thread; nudging it is pestering somebody who has not had the chance to read you yet.

Keep it on the same thread

A follow-up in a fresh thread reads as a second stranger writing about the same job. Threaded, it reads as the same person being reasonable.

What a nudge should say

Morning — just bumping this in case it got buried. Still very interested in the Maintenance Technician role, and happy to come to you any day this week. Harry

No new pitch, no apology for writing, no second question. The first email made the case; this one only makes it visible again.

The whole sequence, with the letters it follows, is in the playbook.
