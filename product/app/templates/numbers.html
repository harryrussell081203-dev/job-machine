What normal looks like

So you can judge the numbers above rather than take a word for them. These are other people's figures, not ours.

Read the third row again. Applying to more jobs made it worse — people who sent over a hundred applications interviewed at less than a third of the rate of people who sent eleven to twenty. Volume is not a strategy that is merely inefficient. It is one that backfires.

Where the founder's own numbers come from

The {% if record %}{{ record.applications }} applications and {{ record.replies }} replies{% else %}applications and replies{% endif %} quoted elsewhere on this site are Harry's, sent by the machine this product was built out of, from his own mailbox, before there was a product. They are counted from his sent folder and his replies — not from this database, which is why they are not in the panel at the top.

They are no longer typed in by hand. That machine writes them out at the end of every run and this page reads what it wrote, so they are the running total on {%- if record and record.updated_at %} {{ record.updated_at[:10] }} {%- endif %} rather than a good fortnight chosen out of it. They will go down as well as up.

They also measure a different thing from the table above. Those are interview rates. His is a reply rate: an answer from a human being at the company. Putting the two side by side as though they were the same number would be dishonest, so they are kept apart.
